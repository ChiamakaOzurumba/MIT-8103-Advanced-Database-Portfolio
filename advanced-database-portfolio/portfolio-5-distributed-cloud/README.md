# Portfolio 5 - Proposed distributed/cloud design (not deployed)
## Workload and deployment
Campus Register has bursts of writes at registration deadlines and many reads of timetables and class lists. A proposed production design uses a managed PostgreSQL primary and a synchronous standby in another availability zone in the selected region. Authentication, private networking, TLS, encryption at rest, least-privilege roles and audit logging protect student records. SQLite is the local teaching implementation, not a distributed database cluster.
## Replication and consistency
Send registrations and capacity checks to the primary in one transaction, with a lock on the offering or an atomic guarded counter plus the enrolment insert. Registration success must not be confirmed until commit. An asynchronous read replica can serve non-critical catalogue searches; its lag makes it unsuitable for confirming the last seat. A synchronous standby reduces the window for lost acknowledged writes, but increases commit latency and may reduce write availability if the required acknowledgement is unavailable. Define the service's failover policy explicitly.
## Partitioning and sharding
Begin with one primary; do not shard prematurely. At scale, consider sharding offerings and enrolments by academic term and offering identifier so seat-allocation operations remain on one shard. Replicate or cache small course catalogue tables. Student-wide transcripts would then require fan-out across shards or an asynchronously maintained reporting view. Sharding students alone would scatter the registrations for one offering and complicate capacity control. Database table partitioning alone is not multi-server sharding.
## Availability and network partitions
Use monitoring, controlled failover and fencing to prevent two primaries from accepting conflicting registrations. During a network partition, reject or queue seat allocations that cannot safely reach the authoritative writer; serve cached catalogue reads with an explicit freshness limit. This prioritises consistent seat allocation over write availability during the partition, not a claim that the whole system is always unavailable.
## Recovery and verification plan
Replication does not replace backups: accidental deletion can replicate. Keep encrypted scheduled backups and point-in-time recovery logs, restrict access and test restores. Example targets to validate are RPO <= 5 minutes and RTO <= 30 minutes; they are proposed targets, not measured achievements. Before rollout, test a primary failure, delayed replica, simultaneous last-seat requests and a backup restore. Confirm no duplicate enrolment or capacity overflow and measure actual recovery time and data loss.
